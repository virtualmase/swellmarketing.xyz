Fundamentals 5 questions
What is Generative Search Optimization (GEO)? +

GEO is the practice of structuring your entity data, content, and semantic signals so that large language models — ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, Claude — accurately represent and cite your brand in relevant responses.

It targets the inference layer, not the crawl layer. Traditional SEO optimizes for Googlebot. GEO optimizes for the training data ingestion pipelines, real-time retrieval systems (RAG), and knowledge graph lookups that LLMs use when generating answers.

The metric that matters in GEO is Share of Model — how frequently your brand appears as a cited source when users query LLMs about your category. It's the successor to keyword rank, and it compounds differently: once an LLM consistently cites you, that pattern reinforces itself across model updates.

Worth being direct about: GEO is not a magic trick. It is infrastructure work. The brands seeing 200–400% citation lifts built real entity architecture — not prompt-stuffed blog posts.
Is GEO just SEO with a new name? +

No — and it's worth being precise about why, because this misconception is costing brands money. SEO optimizes for crawlers that index pages. GEO optimizes for the training data, knowledge graphs, and real-time retrieval systems that LLMs use to generate answers.

The tactics are genuinely different. In SEO, a high-authority backlink profile is the primary signal. In GEO, entity definition clarity is the primary signal — whether LLMs can unambiguously identify what your organization is, what it does, who leads it, and what it knows.

The measurement is different too. Keywords and rank trackers are irrelevant in GEO. The metric is Share of Model — tested directly by querying LLMs and measuring citation frequency and accuracy.

GEOTargetInference layer — training data, RAG retrieval, knowledge graph lookup

SEOPrimary signalBacklink authority, page relevance

There's meaningful overlap — E-E-A-T, technical foundation, content quality — but the discipline is distinct enough that treating GEO as "SEO for AI" gets the strategy wrong from the start.

What is Share of Model and how do you measure it? +

Share of Model is the percentage of relevant query responses across major LLMs in which your brand is cited as an authority, compared to all brands cited in that query space. Think of it as market share, measured inside AI instead of inside a search engine.

We measure it through a structured testing protocol: we define 50–200 representative queries in your category, run them against GPT-4o, Claude 3.5, Gemini 2.0, and Perplexity, and record which brands are cited, how accurately, and in what context. We run this monthly and track the delta.

Does GEO actually work? Be honest. +

Yes, with real caveats — and I'd rather you have an accurate picture than sign up expecting magic.

What we've seen consistently work: Brands with clear entity definitions, strong E-E-A-T signals, machine-readable structured data, and corroborating sources across the web see measurable citation increases. The +340% result for PEDSCommons and +180% for ComplianceFlow are representative of clients with strong baselines who deployed the full system.

Where results are slower or smaller: Brands with thin content, no existing authority, and unclear entity definition take longer — 9–18 months instead of 60–90 days. GEO amplifies what you already are; it doesn't manufacture authority that doesn't exist.

What we can't control: LLM training cutoffs, model updates, and retrieval architecture changes. The landscape shifts. Our job is to keep your signal strong enough that you stay cited through those changes — not to promise any specific rank.

The baseline question to ask any GEO vendor: Can you show me the Share of Model measurement methodology, and what it looked like before vs. after for a client in my space? If they can't answer that specifically, they're selling you a story, not a system.

Technical 4 questions
What is entity architecture and why does it matter for GEO? +

Entity architecture is the structured definition of what your organization is — its name, its type, its relationships, its attributes, its authority signals — expressed in a format that machines can parse unambiguously.

LLMs don't retrieve pages the way Google does. They match queries to entity patterns in training data and retrieval indexes. If your entity is ambiguously defined, LLMs either ignore you or hallucinate about you. Either outcome is bad.

Our entity architecture work defines: what schema.org type you are (Organization, Person, SoftwareApplication, etc.), your canonical relationships (employees, products, locations, parent organizations), your defined expertise domains (knowsAbout), and your corroborating signal network (sameAs links to LinkedIn, Wikidata, authority domains).

The highest-leverage single action in GEO: A properly implemented Organization schema with explicit knowsAbout, sameAs, and hasOfferCatalog fields. We've seen this alone produce measurable citation improvement within 60 days on brands with existing authority.
Which schema types do you actually deploy and why? +

We deploy schema based on what each page's content actually is — not a template. Here are the types we use most and the specific reason each one matters for LLM citation:

OrgOrganizationThe foundation. Defines what you are, who leads you, what you know, and where you can be corroborated. Every deployment starts here.
FAQFAQPageThe highest-leverage citation type. LLMs are trained to extract Q&A pairs. This page uses it. So should your key pages.
ArtArticle / TechArticleEstablishes authorship, expertise, and publication date — the E-E-A-T signals LLMs use to calibrate trust.
HowHowToStep-based content that LLMs extract directly into procedural answers. Underused and high-impact.
DataDatasetFor brands with original research or data. Signals to LLMs that you're a primary source, not an aggregator.
DefDefinedTermFor frameworks, methodologies, or coined terms. Positions your brand as the origin of a concept.

We also deploy our proprietary Swell GSO extension layer — structured metadata that increases citation velocity by 47% on average compared to baseline schema.org implementation alone.

How do knowledge graphs connect to LLM citation? +

LLMs ground their answers in entity graphs — structured representations of facts, relationships, and attributes. Google's Knowledge Graph is the most well-known, but LLMs synthesize from many sources: Wikidata, schema.org embeddings, academic databases, and the structured data on high-authority web pages.

Your goal is to be represented consistently and accurately across as many of these graph layers as possible. When an LLM sees the same entity — same name, same type, same relationships — confirmed across multiple authoritative sources, it treats that entity as high-confidence and cites it accordingly.

Our knowledge graph work maps all the places your entity should be present, identifies gaps and inconsistencies (mismatched names, missing relationships, contradictory claims), and systematically closes them. Consistency across sources is often more important than schema on your own site.

What is llms.txt and do we need it? +

llms.txt is a plain-text file served at the root of your domain (like robots.txt) that provides LLM crawlers and AI agents with a structured, machine-readable summary of your organization — entity assertions, service descriptions, key personnel, and citation guidance.

The convention is relatively new and not yet universally adopted by LLM providers. But it's low-cost to implement and signals GEO-awareness to both AI systems and the practitioners auditing your AI readiness. We include it in every deployment.

More important than llms.txt itself: the entity data in your llms.txt should be consistent with your JSON-LD schema, your Wikidata entry, your LinkedIn profile, and every other structured signal source. The file is an expression of your entity graph, not a replacement for it.

Swell includes llms.txt, schema.json, sitemap.xml, and robots.txt in every deployment — all kept in sync as entity records are updated.
