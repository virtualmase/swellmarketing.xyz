## Interpretation

The segment is evidence-rich: recent B2B software transitions create inspectable identity and category questions. The research method is also producing clear account records. The current bottleneck is distribution permission, not account relevance or message volume.

Sending unsolicited sequences to named people would invalidate the campaign's consent boundary and provide weak evidence about demand. The correct intervention is to create contexts in which relevant buyers can opt in, respond, or receive an introduction.

### 2. Referral request

Ask existing professional contacts for introductions only when they plausibly know a marketing, product-marketing, communications, or brand owner who has managed a recent company/product transition. Do not send a target-account list or imply those accounts have a problem.

Suggested private request:

> I am researching what happens to buyer-facing answers after a software company changes its name, domain, or category. I have published the verification method without naming companies or alleging errors. If you know a B2B marketing or communications leader currently managing that transition, would you be comfortable forwarding the article or making an introduction? No list upload or automated follow-up.

### 3. Event/community route

Use an existing event, webinar, founder community, product-marketing group, or consented research conversation to ask one question:

> After your last rebrand or category change, did your team preserve what AI-assisted buyer research said before and after the launch?

Invite respondents with a specific consequence to the diagnostic. Do not turn community membership into permission for direct sales messages.

### 4. Partner route

Prioritize brand studios, rebrand agencies, PR/communications firms, web migration specialists, and product-marketing consultants. Their clients experience the trigger, and an introduction can transfer context legitimately. A partner brief must explain fit, non-promises, the diagnostic, private Baseline scoping, and attribution.
