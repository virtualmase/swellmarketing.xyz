## Offer architecture

### Positioning sentence

Swell establishes what selected AI products say about a company under documented conditions, connects commercially material claims to inspectable evidence, locates the earliest controllable constraint, and turns the finding into an owned operating decision.

### Buyer-language version

Bring one answer that is costly to leave wrong, outdated, or unsupported. Swell will determine whether the first problem is identity, access, evidence, corroboration, or measurement, then scope only the work the record supports.

### Why the buyer acts now

The trigger is not “AI is important.” It is a current decision with a consequence:

- A rebrand, launch, merger, or category change is being described using the prior state.
- A priority buyer question produces an inaccurate, incomplete, or weakly supported answer.
- Products, executives, locations, or sibling brands are conflated.
- A sales, product marketing, communications, or leadership team cannot distinguish an observed answer from an anecdote because no versioned baseline exists.

### What the baseline buys

The proposal must translate the baseline into inspectable artifacts, not “insights”:

- Approved entity, market, and priority-question scope
- Versioned query and observation record
- Claim-to-source and explicit-unknown record
- Earliest-constraint finding with evidence
- Prioritized corrective roadmap with owners and dependencies
- Decision review that determines self-guided closure, managed scope, or no further work

Acceptance means the agreed artifacts exist, are versioned, and have been reviewed. It never means a third-party answer changed.

### Growth and Scale choice

Recommend Growth when one primary entity or focused workstream has a credible baseline, one named reviewer, and a monthly decision rhythm. Recommend Scale when the approved program genuinely spans multiple entities, markets, workstreams, or cross-functional approvers. Do not use Scale as an arbitrary “more deliverables” upsell.

## Ideal account and contact filter

Research an account only when there is a dated, first-party trigger within roughly 24 months. Prioritize B2B software and technical services where AI-assisted buyer research is plausible. Capture:

- Old state, current state, effective date, and first-party source
- One buyer question that could expose the transition
- Plausible commercial consequence, clearly labeled as an inference
- Marketing, product marketing, communications, growth, or founder owner
- Contact basis, suppression status, and allowed channel

Public relevance is not contact permission. Do not claim an account has an AI representation problem until an observed answer has been preserved under documented conditions.

## Campaign message ladder

1. **Observation:** Your public record changed from X to Y on a documented date.
2. **Uncertainty:** We have not established that AI products describe it inaccurately.
3. **Decision:** If the old state still appears in buyer research, the costly issue is the mismatch, not a generic visibility score.
4. **Method:** Preserve the answer and source trail, identify the earliest constraint, then choose the corrective work.
5. **Action:** Run the diagnostic or reply with one observed answer. A working session is for a live commercial decision.

## Discovery conversion guide

### Opening contract

“By the end of 30 minutes, we should choose one of five outcomes: self-guided, paid baseline, managed-scope preparation, permission-based nurture, or close. I will not turn an assumption into scope. Does that work?”

### Required questions

Ask in this order. Do not spend the call teaching GEO before the buying condition is real.

1. “What exact answer or representation caused this conversation?”
2. “Where, when, and under what query or conditions did you observe it?”
3. “Which buyer, launch, pipeline stage, reputation issue, or operating decision does it affect?”
4. “What happens if it remains unresolved for the next 90 days?”
5. “Which company, product, person, location, market, and language are actually in scope?”
6. “What source should a reviewer trust as the current state, and who can validate it?”
7. “Do you already have a versioned query set, preserved answers, citations, dates, and comparison conditions?”
8. “Who owns the decision, evidence, technical changes, and publishing approval?”
9. “What has to happen before a scope can be accepted, and by what date?”
10. “The public managed programs begin at $2,500/month. Is that investment range workable if the evidence supports recurring work?”

### Diagnose before prescribing

Summarize: “You observed [fact]. It affects [buyer-confirmed consequence]. [Known evidence] supports the current state. [Unknown] remains unresolved. The earliest apparent constraint is [layer], subject to [verification].” Ask the buyer to correct it.

### Routing rules

- **Self-guided:** No material consequence, team can operate the diagnostic, or current evidence is sufficient for an internal decision.
- **Baseline:** Material consequence exists, but no credible versioned observation/evidence record exists.
- **Managed-scope preparation:** Credible baseline exists, recurring corrective work is defined, owners can approve it, and the published investment range fits.
- **Nurture:** Plausible fit, explicit follow-up permission, and a named timing trigger.
- **Close/disqualify:** Guarantee seeking, fabricated authority, no evidence owner, unrelated scope, no consequence, or no viable timing/investment path.

### End-of-call close

“My recommendation is [path] because [evidence]. The next decision is [decision], owned by [person], on [date]. If we cannot put that step on the calendar now, I will record this as nurture or closed rather than pretend it is active pipeline.”

## Objection handling

### “Can you guarantee citations, rankings, or leads?”

“No. Those outcomes are controlled by third parties and buyer behavior. We can contract for the evidence, access, structured-data parity, versioned observations, corrective work, and review cadence under our control. If a guaranteed platform outcome is required, Swell is not a fit.”

### “Why pay for a baseline instead of starting the work?”

“Because publishing before locating the earliest constraint can enlarge the record without resolving identity, access, or evidence. The baseline is the scope-control mechanism. If you already have a credible equivalent record, we will review it rather than sell it again.”

### “Is this just SEO?”

“It overlaps with technical access and source quality, but the unit of work is the evidence-backed representation of defined entities across selected answer conditions. The operating record includes what was asked, observed, sourced, changed, and reviewed. It is not a keyword-volume package.”

### “Why not use our current agency or internal team?”

“You may be able to. The fit question is whether that team already owns the versioned query record, claim-to-source evidence, earliest-constraint diagnosis, implementation approvals, and re-observation cadence. If it does, self-guided or internal execution may be the right decision.”

### “Why is the baseline price private?”

“The public site does not sell a preset baseline. The number of entities, markets, questions, answer surfaces, and evidence sources changes the work. Swell states the boundary, artifacts, acceptance criteria, dependencies, and price in writing before collection.”

### “Why a monthly engagement?”

“Use monthly work only when approved claims, sources, journeys, entities, or observed answers require an operating cadence. A finite problem should receive a finite scope. Recurring work is not the default merely because it is available.”

### “We need a free audit first.”

“The ungated diagnostic is the free orientation. A company-specific evidence record requires scoped research and review. The fit review decides whether that commissioned record is necessary; it is not a disguised free audit.”

### “$2,500 per month is too high.”

“Then we should not hide the mismatch inside a smaller undocumented package. We can identify the highest-priority constraint, route to self-guided work, or close and revisit when the commercial consequence and operating capacity support the published range.”

### “Send a proposal.”

“I can send one after we confirm the problem statement, evidence boundary, decision process, owner, and decision-review date. Otherwise the document would be a price sheet, not a scope.”

## Proposal conversion improvements

Use `docs/PROPOSAL_TEMPLATE.md`, with these required additions for every live proposal:

1. Put a one-sentence buyer decision at the top: “Approve [engagement] to resolve/verify [constraint] for [entities/questions] before [business date].”
2. Separate **observed facts**, **buyer statements**, **seller inferences**, and **unknowns**. This prevents sales language from becoming delivery truth.
3. Include a scope counter: number of entities, markets/languages, priority questions, answer surfaces, canonical sources, and review rounds.
4. Show a deliverable-to-acceptance table. Each fee must buy inspectable completion evidence.
5. Add “not included” beside each phase, not only in a distant exclusions section.
6. Name buyer dependencies with due dates and the schedule consequence of delay.
7. Present one recommended scope. Include an alternative only when it represents a genuinely different decision, such as baseline versus internal/self-guided. Do not manufacture three tiers.
8. State recurring conversion separately: completing a baseline does not automatically enroll the buyer in Growth or Scale.
9. Schedule the decision review before sending. Put its date in the email and proposal.
10. Use a seven-to-fourteen-day expiration aligned to the buying process. Expiration is capacity and evidence control, not artificial urgency.

### Proposal review agenda

1. Ask the buyer to restate the decision and consequence.
2. Confirm the fact/inference/unknown record.
3. Review scope counters and exclusions before deliverable detail.
4. Confirm acceptance evidence and buyer dependencies.
5. Resolve commercial and procurement questions.
6. Choose proceed, named revision, dated defer, or close.

## Funnel objections and conversion repairs

| Friction | Current evidence | Conversion repair |
|---|---|---|
| Baseline pricing must remain scope-specific | Public pages and canonical model keep it private | Quote only through an approved written proposal and state that baseline completion does not auto-enroll. |
| “GEO fit review,” “working session,” and “book” describe the same calendar step differently | Public CTAs use all three terms | Internally normalize the event as a 30-minute fit-review working session; preserve the page CTA until a public-copy change is separately authorized. |
| Diagnostic can end without a captured next step | It is deliberately ungated | At the result, sales follow-up should be driven only by a separate request or consented interaction; measure diagnostic-to-request, not anonymous completions as leads. |
| Services page explains artifacts but not the commercial choice | It routes to diagnostic and pricing | Use the Growth/Scale choice rules in discovery and proposal; do not invent service bundles in outreach. |
| No client proof may be fabricated and current campaign evidence is a public-search control, not AI-answer evidence | Repository claim boundary | Sell inspectable method, boundaries, and sample artifacts. Do not convert the control observation into a performance claim. |
| Broad “B2B software, professional services, technical companies” language weakens outbound relevance | Operating model contains a broad primary segment | Initial account work should use dated transition triggers; review qualification by trigger type before expanding. |

## CRM and stage discipline

Minimum fields by sales motion:

- Outreach research: account, dated trigger, source URL, contact basis, suppression result, owner
- Connected: buyer-stated trigger, consequence, observed surface/query, next action/date
- Qualified: seven-dimension evidence, earliest constraint, evidence owner, investment acknowledgement, recommended path
- Proposal: canonical offer, one-time versus recurring, amount, version, decision date, decision-review meeting, expiration
- Closed lost: controlled loss reason plus buyer language

Do not advance a stage because an email was sent. Do not leave a proposal open without a future buyer-confirmed action.

## Weekly conversion review

Review these counts and the language behind them:

- Permitted contextual messages sent
- Positive replies and specific observed problems
- Connected conversations
- Qualified working sessions booked and held
- Paid baselines proposed and sold
- Growth/Scale proposals and wins
- Median days between connected, discovery, proposal, and decision
- Loss reasons, no-shows, opt-outs, and records without next actions

The first optimization question is where qualified buyers stop. Change the message when replies lack consequence. Change targeting when evidence access or authority repeatedly fails. Change proposal structure when qualified opportunities stall after scope. Do not compensate for a conversion problem by increasing unqualified volume.
