Guide · Entity optimization

One real entity. One coherent record.

Entity optimization makes companies, people, products, and relationships easier to distinguish and verify across owned pages, structured data, and relevant external sources.

Entity optimization is the work of making an organization, person, product, location, or concept unambiguous and consistently connected to supportable attributes and relationships.

A brand can publish accurate sentences while still presenting an incoherent identity. A renamed company may retain the old description. A founder and agency may be treated as the same entity. A product may inherit claims belonging to its parent. Entity work resolves those boundaries before a content program amplifies the conflict.

Entity clarity before keyword volume

Entity clarity asks whether the intended subject can be reliably distinguished from similarly named, historical, parent, sibling, partner, and author entities. It depends on consistent facts, not repeating a name more often.

Build an entity architecture

An entity architecture is the governed map behind public descriptions and markup. For each material entity, record:

  • A stable internal ID and public canonical URL.
  • The preferred name plus legitimate alternate names.
  • A concise, evidence-backed description and current category.
  • Verified attributes, owners, sources, and review dates.
  • Typed relationships to other entities.
  • External profiles that truly represent the same entity.
  • Known conflicts, retired claims, and correction status.

The visible About, service, product, author, and location pages should agree with this record. Structured data should mirror the visible claim rather than introduce a second, invisible version.

What structured data can do

Structured data expresses page meaning in a machine-readable vocabulary. It can clarify that a page describes an Organization, Person, Service, Article, or another supported type, and it can attach verified properties and relationships.

Structured data does not make an unsupported claim true. It does not guarantee that a search or answer product will adopt the markup, create a knowledge-panel-like result, or cite the page. The quality standard is parity: the material structured claim must be visible, current, and supported on the page or an appropriate canonical source.

Use sameAs narrowly

The Schema.org sameAs property points to another URL representing the same entity. It should not point from an organization to its founder, parent, sibling brand, customer, cohort, or partner simply because the two are related.

Entity optimization workflow

  1. Inventory. List the entities that materially affect buyer understanding.
  2. Select canonical sources. Assign one preferred page and stable ID per entity.
  3. Reconcile conflicts. Compare owned pages, metadata, markup, public profiles, and important external descriptions.
  4. Define relationships. Type each connection instead of relying on proximity or vague language.
  5. Repair visible pages. Update human-readable claims before or with markup.
  6. Publish matching structured data. Use the most specific supported types and properties.
  7. Verify retrieval and rendering. Test actual HTML, status, canonicalization, links, and structured-data validity.
  8. Observe answers. Include branded and disambiguation questions in the query portfolio.

Quality checks

  • Every material entity has one stable canonical ID and page.
  • Names, categories, relationships, and current offers agree across important owned surfaces.
  • Visible copy and structured data express the same material facts.
  • sameAs targets have been manually verified as the same entity.
  • Old entities and products are redirected, archived, or explicitly related, not silently erased.
  • External corrections are factual and evidence-backed.
  • Answer observations distinguish identity errors from evidence or retrieval problems.
