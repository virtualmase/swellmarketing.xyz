Pillar guide · AI SEO strategy

Build the record.Then ask to be found.

AI SEO is not a bag of new search tricks. It is the work of making the right page easy to crawl, useful to read, clear about its claims, and connected to the rest of the site.

AI SEO strategy is the discipline of making public information discoverable, understandable, supportable, and current so a search system can evaluate it and a buyer can inspect it. The work starts with search fundamentals. It does not start with a special AI file or a new schema type.

A buyer asks an answer engine whether a product fits their use case. The system finds an old product page, a thin comparison page, and a help article written before the product changed. That is not an AI problem. It is a public-record problem.

The fix is rarely “publish more.” First, decide which page should carry the answer. Then make that page easy to discover, specific enough to help, and clear about what supports its claims. That is a strategy. The rest is implementation.

The foundation still matters

Google says that the same foundational SEO practices apply to AI Overviews and AI Mode. A page must be indexed and eligible to appear in Google Search with a snippet before it can be eligible as a supporting link in those experiences. Google does not list additional technical requirements for that eligibility. [1]

Bing makes the same point for Bing, Copilot, and grounding experiences. Crawl efficiency, indexing accuracy, URL consolidation, content clarity, structure, authority, and trust signals remain the base layer. [3]

A working AI SEO strategy

Use a sequence that begins with a decision, not a keyword list. The goal is to make a specific answer more useful and easier to inspect. A page may still not rank, be surfaced, or be cited. The sequence improves the condition you control.

1. Choose the decision before the query

Start with the decision the buyer is trying to make. “Can this integrate with our current system?” and “Is this product available now?” are better starting points than “AI SEO keywords.” They point to a real page, a real source, and a real owner.

2. Assign one canonical page

Pick the page that owns the answer. Do not create five near-identical pages for small phrasing changes. Google explicitly says there is no requirement to rewrite content for every AI query variation, and that high page volume does not make a site more relevant. [2]

3. Put the important answer near the top

Name the subject. Answer the question. State the scope. Then provide the detail. Bing recommends a focused URL, direct early information, and explicit facts that do not depend on implied context. [3]

4. Keep the source next to the claim

Use documentation, a disclosed method, a primary source, or a clear limitation. Do not send a buyer to a search result and call that proof. When structured data is useful, make it match the visible page content. Google advises accurate, complete markup that describes the page it appears on. [4]

5. Make discovery boring and dependable

Use stable URLs, crawlable internal links, an up-to-date XML sitemap, and consistent canonicalization. Bing recommends these directly for discovery, freshness, URL consolidation, and grounding eligibility. [3]

6. Review the page when the condition changes

Record what changed, who approved it, and when the page should be checked again. An update has value when it keeps the public record accurate, not when it creates another excuse to refresh the date.

What to skip

A sound AI SEO strategy has fewer moving parts than the market suggests. Several popular tactics are either unsupported or distract from the work that matters.

ShortcutWhat the guidance saysBetter use of time
Special AI markup or a new file for Google AI featuresGoogle says there is no special markup, AI text file, or new machine-readable file required for its generative AI features. [2]Confirm the visible page is useful, crawlable, and eligible for Search.
Breaking every page into tiny “AI chunks”Google says there is no chunking requirement and no ideal page length. [2]Structure the page for the reader’s actual question.
Writing a page for every long-tail variationGoogle warns against producing content primarily to manipulate rankings or generative responses. [2]Create a new page only when the question, decision, or evidence changes.
Adding schema that says more than the pageGoogle says markup should describe the content of the page and not invisible or unsupported information. [4]Fix the visible record first. Then add accurate markup where it helps.

Swell publishes an llms.txt inventory for its own discovery and clarity use case. That file is not presented here as a requirement for Google AI features. Treat it as a site-owned reference surface, not a shortcut to selection.

Measure the condition, not a fantasy

Do not treat a single screenshot or one answer as proof that an intervention worked. Track the conditions you can document: whether the URL was live, whether the content changed, whether the source was visible, whether the canonical page was included in the sitemap, and whether the answer observation occurred under known conditions.

For Google, Search Console is the place to inspect indexing and aggregate search performance. Google reports AI-feature traffic within its Search Console reporting and recommends URL Inspection for technical diagnosis. [1] For Bing and Copilot, Bing Webmaster Tools provides the corresponding search and AI-performance context. [3]

Use a Representation Baseline to log the question, condition, answer, source link, date, and reviewer. Use an Answer Change Log when you want to compare observations after a documented page change. This is slower than declaring a win. It is more useful.

What to do this week

  1. Choose one costly buyer question. Pick a question that is already influencing a sales conversation, support ticket, or product comparison.
  2. Audit the current answer page. Check the canonical URL, main claim, source, owner, and review date.
  3. Fix the earliest visible gap. A missing source or contradictory product state is usually more important than another supporting article.
  4. Confirm technical basics. Verify crawl access, internal links, sitemap inclusion, visible text, and accurate structured data.
  5. Record the before state. Keep the query, conditions, screenshot, public answer, and page version together.

The page that earns attention is not the one with the most AI vocabulary. It is the page that lets a person get to a grounded answer with less guesswork.
