Transition field guide · Updated August 19, 2026

Change the brand. Preserve the record.

A name or domain launch can be correct while buyer-facing systems still encounter old identities, broken source paths, or unsupported new claims. Use this checklist to make the transition inspectable before, during, and after launch.

Five control points

The transition checklist.

Assign an owner and retain acceptance evidence for each item. “Published” is an activity; “verified under named conditions” is a state.

01 · Define

Freeze the transition record

Record the former and current names, domains, products, effective date, legal relationship, approved one-sentence description, affected audiences, and accountable approver. Keep one canonical transition brief.

02 · Identify

Preserve identity continuity

Update visible identity, organization and product markup, canonical URLs, sameAs references, author and publisher identifiers, contact details, and former-name language. Do not declare unrelated brands or people as the same entity.

03 · Migrate

Move access and evidence

Map old URLs to the closest current sources, test redirects and canonicals, retain material historical evidence, update internal links, render important claims visibly, and verify robots, sitemap, status codes, and analytics on the destination.

04 · Reconcile

Correct governed external sources

List profiles, directories, partner pages, press materials, documentation, and corroborating sources that matter to the buyer. Update only sources you control or have permission to correct; record conflicting or unavailable sources as constraints.

05 · Observe

Measure selected buyer questions

Choose a stable query set, capture dated answer conditions and cited sources, distinguish inclusion from accuracy, and compare later observations without claiming the migration caused a third-party system to change.

Minimum handoff packet

Give the next owner evidence.

A useful handoff can fit in six governed records.

Identity

Transition map

Old and new names, domains, products, relationships, effective dates, and canonical identifiers.

Claims

Approved source set

Current claims, visible proof, source URLs, limitations, approvers, and review dates.

Technical

Migration test record

Redirect samples, canonical checks, status codes, rendered content, structured-data parity, robots, and sitemap verification.

External

Source reconciliation log

Owned and independent sources, correction status, permission boundary, conflicts, and next owner.

Observation

Representation baseline

Versioned questions, conditions, answers, citations, accuracy notes, and explicit unknowns.

Decision

Constraint register

The earliest unresolved layer, commercial consequence, accountable owner, due date, and acceptance evidence.

Launch gate

Do not call it complete when…

The former identity simply disappears

Buyers and systems may still need an explicit, dated relationship between the former and current entities.

Redirects replace a source strategy

A redirect moves a request. It does not prove the destination visibly supports the current claim.

Markup contradicts the page

Structured data should identify and organize visible material, not carry claims a reviewer cannot inspect.

One answer becomes the KPI

A single screenshot lacks a denominator, repeatable conditions, and enough evidence for a causal conclusion.

Claim boundary

Completing this checklist improves controllable identity, access, evidence, source, and measurement conditions. It does not guarantee ranking, citation, inclusion, traffic, leads, revenue, or a particular third-party answer.
