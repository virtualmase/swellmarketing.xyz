Generative engine optimization (GEO) is the practice of improving the clarity, accessibility, evidence, and corroboration that generative answer systems can use when representing an entity or topic.

Buyers increasingly ask AI-assisted products to explain categories, compare vendors, summarize companies, and verify claims. The output may contain a correct description, a dated description, a misattributed fact, an unsupported recommendation, a competitor list, or no mention at all. GEO treats those outcomes as observations to investigate, not as a hidden score that can be manipulated directly.

What GEO is not

GEO is not a guarantee that a model will rank, mention, recommend, or cite a company. It is not bulk content production with “AI” added to the label. It is not schema markup alone, a special file alone, or a prompt-writing trick.

The useful work occurs where a supportable company record meets a reproducible answer observation. When the two differ materially, there is a representation gap. The job is to find the earliest verified cause within the organization’s control.

The five operating layers

LayerQuestionTypical work
Entity definitionIs the intended organization, person, product, or relationship unambiguous?Canonical identifiers, entity map, ownership and relationship correction
AccessCan relevant systems reliably retrieve the current public evidence?Responses, rendering, internal links, sitemaps, directives, crawl verification
EvidenceAre material claims explicit, scoped, current, and supportable?Claim-to-source mapping, canonical pages, content repair, provenance
CorroborationDo appropriate independent sources support important external claims?Source accuracy, public profiles, earned authority, legitimate correction
MeasurementCan answer changes be observed under documented conditions?Query portfolio, baseline, answer capture, review cadence, change log

Sequence matters. More content cannot reliably repair an unresolved identity conflict. More outreach cannot turn an unsupported claim into evidence. More screenshots cannot create a controlled baseline after the fact.

GEO, SEO, and AEO

PracticePrimary lensShared ground
SEOSearch discovery, rankings, qualified visits, and site performanceTechnical access, content quality, authority, structured data, measurement
AEOClear resolution of questions in search and answer interfacesQuestion structure, direct answers, entity clarity, evidence
GEORepresentation, inclusion, attribution, and citation in generative answersAll of the above, plus versioned answer observation

The practices overlap. A technically healthy, useful site remains foundational. GEO adds a specific concern: what generative products actually say, under which conditions, and whether that answer matches the evidence.

A governed GEO workflow

  1. Define the buyer questions. Build a stable portfolio across category, problem, comparison, and verification intent.
  2. Capture a baseline. Preserve the product, mode, query, date, answer, citations, named alternatives, and relevant conditions.
  3. Map claims to evidence. Separate what is supported, ambiguous, outdated, missing, or independently corroborated.
  4. Identify the first constraint. Prioritize the earliest layer that can materially explain the gap.
  5. Ship a bounded intervention. Give the work an owner, scope, acceptance criteria, and change record.
  6. Observe again. Compare later answers without turning correlation into unsupported causality.

Swell uses a Representation Baseline, Evidence Ledger, and Answer Change Log to make that workflow inspectable.

What to measure

Separate answer dimensions instead of collapsing them into one vanity score:

  • Inclusion: whether the intended entity appears.
  • Accuracy: whether material claims match current evidence.
  • Attribution: whether claims belong to the correct entity.
  • Citation coverage: whether specified owned or independent sources are referenced.
  • Alternative presence: which other entities appear for the same question set.
  • Commercial action: whether qualified buyers reach the next appropriate step.

The AI visibility guide explains query portfolios, Share of Model, citation coverage, and change logging in more detail.

Where to start

Start with a specific observed problem: a retired product description, a missing category association, a conflated entity, an unsupported claim, or a measurement blind spot. Preserve the evidence before prescribing a channel or deliverable.
